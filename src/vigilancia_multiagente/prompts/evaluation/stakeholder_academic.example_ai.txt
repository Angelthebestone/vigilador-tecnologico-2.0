The report draws structural conclusions from a thin, self-selected evidence base: 14 public statements from neobanks and 6 vendor whitepapers do not constitute a sampling frame from which regional adoption trajectories can be inferred. The reliance on earnings-call declarations introduces a documented bias in which firms over-report forward-looking initiatives, and the absence of any methodology section makes the inclusion/exclusion criteria for the 14 banks unverifiable. The confidence figure of 0.78 is assigned without any stated derivation procedure, suggesting it is editorial rather than computed. Effect sizes (the 60–80% HSM overhead range) are reported without confidence intervals, sample sizes, or workload specification, conflating vendor marketing with peer-reviewed benchmark data.

The leap from "9 of 14 declared a roadmap" to a regional acceleration thesis is an instance of the planning fallacy compounded by survivorship bias: firms still operating in 2024 are precisely those whose strategic narratives survived selection, and the report does not consider neobanks that exited the market or that publicly deprioritized PQC. The horizon (2026–2028) is short enough that adoption claims should be falsifiable, yet no leading indicator is proposed.

- Selection bias in the corpus: 14 self-selected public declarations are not a probabilistic sample of the regional fintech population; the report must specify the sampling frame, response rate, and missing-data handling, or downgrade its claims accordingly.
- Vendor-source contamination: the 60–80% HSM overhead figure originates in supplier whitepapers with commercial incentives. Independent benchmarks (e.g., from BSI, ANSSI, or academic groups) should be cited or the figure flagged as low-quality evidence.
- Absent counterfactual literature: the report does not engage with PQC-deployment studies from EU banks (e.g., ECB TIBER-EU PQC pilots) that report substantially different overhead profiles, indicating publication bias toward optimistic regional outcomes.
- Confidence calibration not derived: a confidence of 0.78 should reference either a Bayesian update procedure, expert-elicitation protocol (e.g., IDEA), or a base-rate-anchored Brier-style calibration, not editorial assertion.
- Missing operational definition of "roadmap in place": without a coded scheme distinguishing announced intent, budgeted initiative, and shipped product, the 9/14 statistic is non-reproducible and effectively unfalsifiable.
