From a public-interest standpoint, the report treats PQC migration primarily as a market opportunity, overlooking the duty-of-care implications that follow once a national or sectoral authority recognizes the harvest-now-decrypt-later threat as a foreseeable risk. The assumption that regional regulators will simply track NIST guidance within 18–24 months understates the heterogeneity of jurisdictional frameworks across Brazil (LGPD, BACEN Resolução 4.658), Mexico (CNBV cybersecurity provisions), Colombia (Ley 1581 de 2012, SFC Circular Externa 007 de 2018), and Chile (CMF NCG and the upcoming Marco de Ciberseguridad). Each of these regimes carries distinct evidentiary and proportionality obligations that the report does not surface, leaving supervised entities exposed to inconsistent compliance posture across markets where they operate simultaneously.

A secondary concern is traceability: the report relies on self-reported "PQC roadmap in place" statements from 9 of 14 surveyed neobanks without any verification mechanism. From a supervisory perspective, those statements are unauditable without a chain-of-custody for the underlying remediation plans, key-rotation schedules, and incident-response playbooks, and they should not be treated as evidence of compliance readiness.

- Jurisdictional uniformity assumption: the report assumes regulators will track NIST within 18–24 months, which is inconsistent with the historical lag observed in BACEN, CNBV, SFC, and CMF crypto-policy diffusion; supervised entities should not rely on a unified timeline for compliance planning.
- Duty of care under harvest-now-decrypt-later: any supervised entity aware of the report's finding cannot later claim absence of foreseeability; this triggers a precautionary obligation that the report should make explicit.
- Performance overhead and consumer access: a 60–80% key-exchange overhead on mobile apps risks disproportionate impact on low-end devices prevalent in the region, raising non-discrimination and digital-rights concerns that an authority would weigh under proportionality.
- Lack of traceable evidence: self-declared "roadmaps" from earnings calls are not auditable artifacts; any binding guidance will require chain-of-custody for remediation plans, key inventories, and rotation schedules.
- Systemic concentration risk: concentrating PQC supply on three regional HSM vendors creates a critical-dependence pattern that competition and operational-resilience authorities would scrutinize under DORA-equivalent frameworks emerging in the region.
- Cross-border data flow framing is incomplete: the report addresses US correspondent banking but omits intra-region transfers and the divergent adequacy regimes that govern them, which is a material gap for any authority issuing binding guidance.
